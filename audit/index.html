§1 — Homepage: the first 10 seconds

BLOCKER

Finding 1.1 — “Fast US Shipping” trust row is not true for most of the catalog

Page: Homepage — https://f6ccps-rz.myshopify.com/ · [screenshot: homepage trust row, captured Oct 14]

Evidence: The homepage trust row reads: “Fast US Shipping — Ships quickly from within the US, straight to your door.”

Why it hurts: Only one product (the wireless charger) is stocked in a US warehouse. The cases, screen protector, and bundle are supplier-fulfilled from overseas. A case buyer reads “ships from within the US,” expects US delivery speed, and gets a longer wait — the classic setup for a dispute and a one-star review.

Fix: Replace the storewide row with wording that is true for every product — e.g. “Tracked shipping on every order — each product page shows its real delivery window” — and state per-product delivery reality on the product pages themselves. (Final wording depends on the store’s fulfillment model per product; a storewide row may only claim what is true storewide.) Fix slot #4 — Standard/Premium.

FIX

Finding 5.1 — The bundle’s saving is stated as three different numbers, and two of them are wrong

Page: https://f6ccps-rz.myshopify.com/products/orrynx-complete-protection-bundle-for-iphone-17-pro-max (plus the cross-sell lines on both case pages and the protector page) · [screenshot: bundle description “20% less” line and protector cross-sell line, captured Oct 14]

Evidence: The bundle costs $18.99 against $24.98 bought separately — a real saving of 24%. But the bundle description opens with “at 20% less than buying separately,” both case pages and the protector page repeat “save 20% with the Complete Protection Bundle,” and the bundle’s own gallery flyers (Finding 6.2) shout “50% OFF.”

Why it hurts: Three different savings claims for one product means at least two are false at any moment. A customer who does the $6 math finds 24% and trusts none of the numbers.

Fix: State one number everywhere — the true one: “Save 24% vs buying separately” — in the description, the cross-sell lines, and any replacement gallery art. Fix slot #8 — Standard/Premium.

Checked — clean: apart from Finding 5.1, all prices and compare-at prices are consistent storewide, in one currency (USD), with no $0.00 or placeholder pricing.

§6 — Claims-vs-reality sweep

This is the highest-value part of the audit — and the part most template audits skip. It holds 4 of this store’s 6 Blockers; the other two are the homepage trust row (Finding 1.1, the same shipping problem) and the branded packaging in the protector gallery (Finding 3.1).

BLOCKER

Finding 6.1 — Storewide “US warehouse / 3–5 day” shipping claims that fulfillment doesn’t support

Page: Shipping policy — https://f6ccps-rz.myshopify.com/policies/shipping-policy · FAQ page (footer link) · the shipping block printed on every product page · [screenshot: shipping policy text, captured Oct 14]

Evidence (three locations, same claim):

  • Shipping policy: “Orders are shipped from our US warehouse within 1–2 business days… delivery typically takes 3–5 business days.”
  • FAQ page, “How long does delivery take?”: the same US-warehouse, 3–5-business-day wording.
  • The shipping block printed on every product page: “Orders ship from our US warehouse within 1–2 business days. Delivery typically takes 3–5 business days after shipping.”

Reality: only the charger is stocked in a US warehouse. Cases, protector, and bundle are supplier-fulfilled from overseas on longer windows — so for 4 of 5 products, both the origin claim and the 3–5-day promise are wrong. (This is the same defect class as the homepage trust row, Finding 1.1 — counted there for the homepage, here for the policy/FAQ/product-page text; each location needs its own rewrite.)

Why it hurts: This is the single most expensive text on the store. It sets a delivery expectation the store cannot meet for most orders — the top driver of “where is my order” tickets, chargebacks, and bad reviews — and it is exactly the kind of claim ad platforms and payment processors treat as deceptive.

Fix: Rewrite all three locations with the true model: processing time, per-product origin, and honest delivery windows (fast US delivery stated only on the charger). One corrected shipping text, reused in the policy, the FAQ, and the product-page block. Fix slots #1–#3 (one per location) — Standard/Premium — ranked fix #1.

Gallery defect group — the bundle’s two supplier flyers (§6.2–§6.5)

The first two images of the bundle gallery are supplier-made promo flyers (files promo-blue-50off.jpg and promo-black-50off.jpg, gallery positions 1–2). They fail the claims sweep in four distinct ways, listed as separate sub-findings because each is a distinct claim failure. One shared fix clears all four: remove both flyers from the gallery and re-lead with the real product photos already in the gallery; if a promo card is wanted, rebuild it with only claims the store can prove (starting with the true 24% saving, Finding 5.1). One fix slot (#5) + the bundle page cleanup — Standard/Premium. (The October 13 pass also noted the two flyers’ color files are crossed relative to the variants they sit under — removal makes that moot.)

BLOCKER

Finding 6.2 — The flyers promise “50% OFF” — three times each

Page: https://f6ccps-rz.myshopify.com/products/orrynx-complete-protection-bundle-for-iphone-17-pro-max (gallery images 1–2) · [screenshot: both flyers at full size, captured Oct 14]

Evidence: Each flyer prints “50% OFF” three times (“SPECIAL OFFER 50% OFF FOR YOU!”, “BUY NOW AND GET 50% OFF THE CASE”, “50% OFF iPhone 17 Pro Max Case”). The bundle’s real saving is 24% (§5).

Why it hurts: A fabricated discount on the product’s own page is a pricing claim the store cannot defend — to customers, to Shopify, or to a regulator.

Fix: Shared gallery fix above — remove both flyers and re-lead with the real product photos; any rebuilt promo card may only claim claims the store can prove, starting with the true 24% saving.

BLOCKER

Finding 6.3 — The flyers sell a privacy feature the product does not have

Page: Same bundle gallery (flyers) · [screenshot: flyer badge area, captured Oct 14]

Evidence: Both flyers carry the badges “ANTI-SPYING — Keeps your screen hidden from side views” and “PRIVACY PROTECTION — Only you can see the screen.”

Reality: the bundled protector is a standard clear nano glass. It has no privacy filter; anyone beside the user can read the screen.

Why it hurts: A feature claim for a feature that doesn’t exist is the clearest form of false advertising — and a guaranteed return reason (“this is not a privacy screen”). No replacement copy may claim privacy filtering.

Fix: Shared gallery fix above — remove both flyers. No replacement copy may claim privacy filtering.

FIX

Finding 6.4 — “*Color will be a surprise!” on a product where the customer picks the color

Why it hurts: It tells the buyer their explicit choice may be ignored. Few lines on a product page do more damage per word: it contradicts the purchase itself.

Fix: Shared gallery fix above — removed with the flyers.

BLOCKER

Finding 6.6 — “7× Military-Grade” and drop-test certification badges with no documentation

Page: Homepage hero + About page — https://f6ccps-rz.myshopify.com/ · both case product pages (gallery badge image) · [screenshot: hero claim + case gallery badge image, captured Oct 14]

Evidence: The homepage hero block and the About page claim “7× Military-Grade Drop Protection”; both case galleries carry a badge image repeating “7x Military Grade Protection” (gallery file orrynx-black-military.jpg / orrynx-blue-military.jpg), alongside supplier badges presenting drop-test / military-standard certification marks.

Reality: the store holds no test report, certification, or supplier documentation for a 7× figure, a military standard, or a drop-test rating. The cases are genuinely reinforced (raised edges, shock-absorbing corners — visible, provable features), but no documented rating exists behind the badges.

Why it hurts: Performance ratings are claims a store must be able to prove on demand — to a customer, a platform, or a regulator. An undocumented “military-grade” multiplier is exactly the kind of claim that gets listings flagged and stores distrusted.

Fix: Remove the “7×” figure and the certification-style badges everywhere; describe only the provable construction (raised 1.5 mm edges, reinforced corners, camera-frame protection). If the supplier ever provides real test documentation, the claim can return — with the document on file. Fix slots #6 (homepage + About text) and #7 (case gallery badge images, both case pages cleaned) — Standard/Premium. A claim is never “fixed” by writing a bigger one.

FIX

Finding 6.7 — The charger’s own shipping line conflicts with the storewide shipping text

Page: https://f6ccps-rz.myshopify.com/products/orrynx-15w-magsafe-wireless-charger · [screenshot: charger description shipping line, captured Oct 14]

Evidence: The charger description states “Ships from our US warehouse — delivery in 3 to 8 days,” while the storewide shipping block printed on the same page (Finding 6.1) promises dispatch in 1–2 business days and delivery in 3–5.

§12 — Fix + re-check protocol (how Standard/Premium would run on this store)

Fixes are implemented in the ranked order of the slot numbers in the tier map below, and every change is logged (what, where, before → after). After fixing, the full customer journey is re-walked — not just the fixed pages, because fixes break neighbors — and the §5 price table and §6 claims sweep are re-run after any price or claim fix. Premium adds the written re-check report with before/after notes per item, and a 14-day follow-up check-in: every item fixed under the order is re-opened and verified; small adjustments to those same items (wording, a price display, a setting) are made free and do not consume a revision; anything new found is logged and quoted separately, never fixed silently. Fixes beyond a tier’s cap are quoted before any work on them — never silently included, never silently dropped.

Tier mapping — which tier fixes what

Scopes are the gig’s real ones. Basic ($80) = this report only (deep-check ≤10 product pages — this 5-product catalog fits entirely). Standard ($150) = up to 10 fixes implemented + up to 5 product pages cleaned (deep-check ≤25). Premium ($250) = up to 20 fixes + up to 15 product pages cleaned (deep-check ≤50), plus the re-check report and the 14-day follow-up. Fix slots are numbered in implementation order; one slot = one implemented fix (the shipping rewrite touches three locations, so it takes three slots; the flyer removal is one slot that clears three findings).
